# Flathire — Brand Positioning Document
*Pre-launch | EMEA & US | Version 1.0*

---

## The one-line truth

**We give fast-growing companies access to engineering-grade talent at a price that doesn't punish them for growing.**

---

## The problem we solve

Traditional recruiting firms charge 15-20% of annual salary per hire. For a company hiring five engineers in a year at $150k each, that's **$112,000–$150,000 in fees**. For a 50-person startup, that's not a recruiting cost. That's a runway problem.

Worse, the model is misaligned. Agencies get paid per placement, so speed beats quality. You get candidates fast. You don't always get the right ones.

The result: founders and CTOs waste hours screening weak pipelines, make expensive mis-hires, and pay a premium for the privilege.

---

## What we do differently

Flathire operates on a fixed monthly retainer based on the number of open roles — not a percentage of salary. One predictable number. No surprises.

But the model isn't the only thing that's different.

Every candidate in our pipeline comes pre-vetted by someone who has actually built engineering teams. Not a recruiter reading a job description. An engineering leader who knows what good looks like from the inside.

We don't send you ten candidates and hope one sticks. We send you three that can do the job.

---

## Who we are for

Companies between 10 and 150 people, Series A through B, or PE-backed, hiring technology talent across the US and EMEA.

Specifically:

- The **founder** who is tired of paying agency fees that eat into runway
- The **CTO** who has wasted two months interviewing candidates who looked good on paper
- The **VP of Engineering** who needs to double the team in six months without doubling the hiring chaos

---

## Who we are not for

Enterprise companies with internal TA teams and structured procurement processes. Companies hiring one role every eighteen months. Anyone who needs volume over quality.

## Our positioning statement

**For fast-growing technology companies that can't afford to mis-hire or overpay to hire, Flathire is the engineering-led talent partner that delivers pre-vetted, shortlist-ready candidates on a fixed monthly fee — replacing the unpredictable cost and inconsistent quality of traditional search firms.**

## The three brand pillars

### 1. Engineering credibility
We come from inside engineering teams, not from recruiting desks. We know what a strong senior backend engineer looks like in a Series B environment. We know the difference between someone who can talk system design and someone who can actually build it. That judgment is baked into every candidate we put forward.

### 2. Radical cost transparency
No percentages. No surprises. No invoice that arrives after the hire and makes your CFO wince. One fixed monthly number tied to your open roles. You know the cost before you sign. You can model it into your runway.

### 3. Shortlist discipline
We do not send pipelines. We send shortlists. Three to five candidates who have been screened, assessed, and cleared before they reach you. Your time is protected. Your interview process is signal, not noise.

## The pricing model

### Starter — $2,500/month
Up to 3 active roles worked simultaneously. For companies in early scaling mode, first engineering hires, focused growth.

### Scale — $4,000/month
Up to 6 active roles worked simultaneously. For companies in active hiring sprints, post Series A/B, PE portfolio companies ramping fast.

### Growth — $4,000/month base + $1,000 per role beyond 6
7+ active roles. Same flat-fee logic, scaled transparently. Example: 9 roles = $7,000/month. Dedicated capacity, embedded hiring partner model, scoped to your roadmap.

*Active roles means roles actively being worked at any given moment. As positions are filled, new roles rotate in — keeping your pipeline continuously moving.*

## Brand kit

These are the exact values used on the live landing page. Use these, not approximations, whenever the brand shows up anywhere else, deck, pitch materials, signature, social.

### Color palette

| Token | Hex / Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Navy Deep | `#0A1628` | Primary background, headlines on light sections |
| Navy Mid | `#1B3A6B` | Section backgrounds, card fills |
| Navy Light | `#243F72` | Hover states, secondary surfaces |
| Orange | `#E8640C` | Primary accent, CTAs, links, highlights |
| Orange Warm | `#F4A261` | Secondary accent, gradients, soft highlights |
| Ice | `#E8EDF5` | Body text on dark backgrounds, light section fills |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Pure contrast, headlines on dark |
| Muted | `#8A9BBF` | Secondary text, captions, labels |
| Border | `rgba(232,237,245,0.12)` | Hairline borders, dividers on dark backgrounds |

**Usage rule:** navy carries authority, orange carries energy and action. Orange is reserved for things the user should notice or act on (CTAs, key numbers, accents). It is never used as a body text color or as a large background fill, that would tip the brand from confident to loud.

**Dark sections** (hero, pillars, calculator, pricing, final CTA): Navy Deep background, Ice body text, White headlines, Orange accents.

**Light sections** (problem statement, how it works): Ice background, Navy Deep headlines, dark gray (`#4A5568`) body copy, Orange accents and eyebrows.

### Typography

| Role | Typeface | Weight(s) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Syne | 700, 800 | Headlines, section titles, plan names |
| Body | Inter | 300, 400, 500, 600 | Paragraph copy, UI text, navigation |
| Data / Mono | IBM Plex Mono | 400, 500 | Numbers, stats, pricing, labels, eyebrows |

**Why this pairing:** Syne is geometric and a little unconventional, it signals precision without looking like a generic SaaS template. Inter is the clean, neutral workhorse for anything read at length. IBM Plex Mono shows up specifically wherever a number needs to feel calculated and exact rather than decorative, this is what gives the brand its engineering-grade credibility in the typography itself.

**Letter spacing:** Headlines run tight (`-0.02em` to `-0.03em`). Eyebrows and mono labels run wide and uppercase (`0.08em` to `0.14em`). This contrast is intentional, it's part of what makes the brand feel engineered rather than templated.

### Logo treatment

Wordmark: **flat**hire, set in Syne 800 weight. "flat" in white or navy depending on background, "hire" in Orange (`#E8640C`). No icon mark currently exists alongside the wordmark, the typography carries the brand on its own.

### Iconography

Line-art SVG icons only, no filled icons, no emoji, no stock illustration. Stroke width 1.5–2px. Primary stroke color Orange, secondary details in Orange Warm. Icons live inside a circle or rounded-square container with a subtle border, never floating unframed. This keeps every icon feeling like part of one consistent system rather than a grab-bag of clipart.

### Spacing and shape language

Border radius: 6–16px depending on element size (buttons tighter, cards looser). Nothing fully square, nothing fully pill-shaped except small tags and badges. Generous whitespace between sections (100px+ vertical padding) to keep the page from feeling crowded, this is part of the premium signal for PE and CFO audiences.

### Motion

Used sparingly and always with purpose, never decorative for its own sake. Float animations on hero cards (slow, 3.5–5s loops) suggest a live, working product rather than a static page. Progress bars and fills animate once on load or scroll to draw the eye to a number that matters. Fade-up on scroll for section entrances. Nothing spins, bounces, or calls excess attention to itself, the energy comes from the orange accent and the copy, not from movement.
